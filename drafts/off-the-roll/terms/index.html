Asked and Paid: Providence’s Agreements on Boston’s Terms

Boston asks its colleges and hospitals for a quarter of the tax their exempt property would owe. Asked that way, on the 2025 roll at Providence’s commercial rate, Providence’s six paying colleges and hospitals would be asked $34.5M a year. Under their agreements they paid $14.2M, 41% of it. Boston asked its 38 institutions $133.6M in fiscal 2025 and was paid $35.5M in cash, 27%; the rest was met in credited community programs or not at all.

How the two cities ask

Boston’s 2010 PILOT Task Force set the terms its recap still reports against: every educational, medical and cultural nonprofit holding more than $15 million in property is asked for a quarter of what the property would owe if taxed, the share of the City budget the Task Force tied to police, fire, snow removal and other basic services; generally up to half of the ask can be met with community programs the City credits; the City sets the value basis and publishes the ask, the cash, the credit and the share met every year. Providence has no formula. Each payment is set by its own agreement: the 2023 twenty-year agreement with Brown, RISD, Providence College and Johnson & Wales fixes every year’s payment through fiscal 2043; Brown’s separate ten-year agreement adds $46 million; the hospitals and the mall have agreements of their own. Stabilization agreements are ordinances, one per project, each with its own schedule. The State reimburses Providence for part of the college and hospital tax it does not collect; Massachusetts does not.

What doesn’t line up

  • The formula columns are arithmetic, not a record: a quarter of exempt value times each city’s commercial rate. Boston itself does not compute its ask on the roll. It computes it on a value basis of its own that runs at about half of roll value, so Boston’s published ask is lower than its own formula on its own roll.
  • Years. Providence’s roll is tax year 2025, after revaluation, at $29.20; its payments are fiscal 2025; the stabilization report is tax year 2024 at $35.10. Boston’s roll is fiscal 2026 at $26.96; its recap is fiscal 2025.
  • Boston’s roll lists some institutional property under names its adapter does not yet resolve, so a Boston row’s exempt value can run low; rows under half the City’s own basis are flagged. Providence’s six match the live Off the Roll page figure for figure.
  • Both cities’ agreements include things this table cannot price: Brown’s credits for development that returns property to the roll, Boston’s community-benefit credits, and the State of Rhode Island’s PILOT payment to Providence.

Not yet on the record

  • The CC-15 “39 New York Avenue (Tangible)” agreement: confirmation from the Internal Auditor of its assessment, bill and full-rate tax.
  • How Boston sets each institution’s value basis, and which year’s values it uses.
  • Brown University Health’s payment for 2026 (none is scheduled), Care New England’s renewal, and whether CharterCare’s hospitals, now nonprofit, have a payment agreement.
