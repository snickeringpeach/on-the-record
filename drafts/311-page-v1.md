## 1. What a 311 is (goes above "The front door")

Most people think a 311 is a number where you complain. That is the first of eight steps. A resident reports something. It gets a category. The category decides which department sees it. The department opens a work order. The order gets a status, a crew, a date. The crew does the work or doesn't. The case closes, with a reason. In a city that publishes its log, all of it becomes a public record: what residents asked for, what the city did, and how long it took. [Link: *Most People Think a 311 Is a Number Where You Complain*, ecoRI News, Oct 12 2026 — https://ecori.org/most-people-think-a-311-is-a-number-where-you-complain/]

I think the desk is larger than that. It runs on the resident's schedule, not the city's, and it is the one place where a single person's need becomes a government action without a ballot, a room, or a lawyer. So it is two things at once: a complaint desk, and a labor market. A resident who can say "the microphone in the council chamber doesn't reach the gallery" can also say "and I'll fix it for $500." Today the desk can hear the first half of that sentence and not the second. [C: the last two sentences compress the ecoRI piece's argument; the frame is yours.]

This page is the home for that argument and for the record it needs. It will outlast any one piece. Below: the case file on the first offer, the City's own numbers on how the desk is used, the log of offers made through it, and, when a city is ready to try it, the pilot. The series that makes the argument in full runs at ecoRI News: *New Deal 2.0*, I (Sept 22) [link], II (Oct 12), III (November), IV (December).
