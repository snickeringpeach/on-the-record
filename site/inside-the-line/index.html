Inside the Line

The Fox Point Hurricane Barrier closes against storm tides for about 280 acres of Providence. This is that line, and what lies on either side of it.

Inside the line are 2% of Providence's residents, 16% of its jobs and 8% of its assessed value. 53% of that value is exempt from the property tax, against 35% citywide. Of the 41 storage tanks mapped in the flood zones, 36 stand outside the line, nearly all along Allens Avenue and Terminal Road, and the Fields Point treatment plant is outside it too. The ground there is lower: on the coastal parcels outside the line, 45% lies below the water Hurricane Bob brought in 1991. Inside, 8%.

What it costs to run

The Army Corps' New England District operates the barrier. The Corps' work plan for fiscal 2026 allots $6,348,000 to Fox Point for operation and maintenance. The President's budget had asked for $698,000; the figure Congress set in its explanatory statement, and the Corps allotted, is nine times that. Beyond routine work, the plan names two jobs: replacing the electrical switchgear and repairing the mechanical systems of the river pumps.

US Army Corps of Engineers, Civil Works, FY 2026 Work Plan, Operation and Maintenance, p. 23. The plan's illustrative breakout puts $2,539,200 of the total to district salaries and expenses and $3,808,800 to the project itself.
