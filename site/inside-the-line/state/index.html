Rhode Island

The state's dams and its flood insurance, town by town.

DEM inventories 669 dams. It classes 94 as High hazard: a failure would mean "probable loss of human life." At the end of 2024 it listed 30 of those as unsafe. Flood-insurance policies statewide fell from 16,217 in 2010, the year of the March floods, to 11,791 in 2024.

Dams

Unsafe, in DEM's words: "an unreasonable risk of failure exists that will result in a probable loss of human life or major economic loss" (p.11). Hazard class is the consequence of a failure, not the dam's condition. The program "no longer has the capacity to perform inspections" (p.35). It did not apply for FEMA's 2024 High Hazard Potential Dams grant "due to lack of staff to apply for and manage the grant" (p.29).

An orphan is a dam "in which no owner can be identified" (p.27); DEM lists two, both High hazard and unsafe. "Owner unknown" means DEM hasn't identified the owner.

Five dams DEM listed were resolved in 2024 and are left out: Wyoming Upper and Jordan (work completed), Hughesdale Upper (notice dismissed on appeal), Rawson Pond (the spillway failed) and Shoestring Mill (reclassified to Low).

73 dams with an open DEM finding at the end of 2024, 32 of them High hazard. A dam on two lists is shown once, under the more serious.
