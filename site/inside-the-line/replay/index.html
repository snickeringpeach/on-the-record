The water at Providence, 1938 to today

Press play. Each step is the high water at the Providence gauge, set against the level where the hurricane barrier’s gates close and the storms on record.

 
 
 
 
Asking NOAA for the water now…

What this shows

  • Before November 1979 the public record gives one figure a month: the highest water that month. From then on it is each day’s high water. The last few weeks are preliminary.
  • Heights are feet above the gauge’s zero (mean lower low water). The Corps closes the gates when the tide is forecast to reach 7.68 feet.
  • Hurricane Carol (1954): the gauge was not recording. Its height is from the CRMC Metro Bay plan.
  • Gate operations are the Army Corps’ summaries for 2010–2025: flood closures, WaterFire closures and tests. Earlier closures are not in the record used here.
  • The blue area on the map is a model, not a record: where water at that height would stand inside the line if the barrier were not there, on today’s ground, up to 12 feet. It has no flow, drains or timing.
  • “Now” is NOAA’s gauge reading when you opened the page. The forecast is the National Weather Service’s for the next three days. Neither is made here.

Sources

NOAA CO-OPS station 8454000 (Providence): verified high and low tides, monthly highest water, 6-minute water levels. National Weather Service, National Water Prediction Service gauge FOXR1. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, Fox Point Hurricane Barrier operation summaries, 2010–2025. USGS 3DEP lidar (the model). FEMA flood zones (the rings).
