What Colleges and Hospitals Pay on Their Exempt Property, in Providence and in Boston

For every $1,000 of exempt property on the tax roll, the six Providence colleges and hospitals paid the City $3.00 in cash in fiscal 2025. Boston’s 38 PILOT institutions paid Boston $1.26. Boston also credits community programs against what it asks; counted that way, its figure is $3.64.

From both cities’ tax rolls, Providence’s payment agreements and Boston’s PILOT recap

These institutions hold $4.72B of exempt property in Providence and $28.09B in Boston. In cash, the Providence institutions paid $14.2M and the Boston institutions $35.5M. Brown paid $11.1M on $1.87B, or $5.93 per $1,000. Of Boston’s four largest holders, Massachusetts General Hospital paid $1.86, Boston University paid $1.53, Harvard University paid $1.10, Northeastern University paid $0.62. Counting Boston’s credits, four Boston institutions come out above Brown’s cash figure: Spaulding Rehabilitation Hospital, Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, GBH (WGBH), Massachusetts Eye & Ear Infirmary.

What Boston asks has risen every year; cash has not. The ask grew from $120.2M to $133.6M, while cash stayed near $35 million. The difference was made up in credits. Six museums left the table in FY2024, when the City moved them to what its recap calls “an alternative system” of reporting community benefits, so the earlier years count more institutions.

The valuation behind the asks has barely moved. For 25 of the 38 institutions listed in all five years, the City’s value basis is the same to the dollar in FY2021 and FY2025: Berklee College of Music at $173.9M, Boston College at $536.1M, Emerson College at $266.7M. Their asks rose about 2.5% a year all the same. The recaps do not say what year’s values the basis comes from.

Since fiscal 2025: two new agreements

  • Boston University, signed June 23, 2026: cash of $6.7M in FY2026 rising to $8.3M in FY2030, $37.5M over the term, plus community benefits valued at $12.7M to $14.0M a year, $66.8M in all. In fiscal 2025 it paid $6.3M in cash against an ask of $24.8M.
  • Wentworth Institute of Technology, signed August 18, 2026: cash of $50,000 in FY2027 rising to $125,000 in FY2031, $400,000 over the term, plus community benefits valued at $982,000 to $1.1M a year, $5.2M in all. In fiscal 2025 it paid $0 in cash against an ask of $1.9M.

Neither agreement mentions a value basis or the quarter-of-the-tax ask. Each sets a fixed schedule of cash, as Providence’s agreements with its colleges do. Boston University’s agreement “shall supersede and replace any and all previous agreements” on payments in lieu of taxes; Wentworth’s replaces its arrangements under the 2010 Task Force report. The table above still shows fiscal 2025, the last year both were under the old program.

How Boston asks

Boston asks every nonprofit holding more than $15 million in property to pay a quarter of what it would owe if the property were taxed, the share of the City’s budget its 2010 PILOT Task Force tied to police, fire, snow removal and other basic services. Generally up to half of that request can be met with community programs the City credits as directly benefiting Boston residents. The City sets each institution’s request on a “value basis” of its own and publishes the request, the cash, the credit and the share met every year. Providence has no formula. Each institution’s payment is set by its own agreement with the City, and the State separately reimburses the City for part of the tax it does not collect from colleges and hospitals. Massachusetts has no such payment for private colleges and hospitals.

What doesn’t line up

  • The two rolls value property as of nearly the same day, Dec. 31, 2024 in Providence and Jan. 1, 2025 in Boston, but under two states’ laws, by two assessors’ offices, with two different practices for exempt property, which neither city taxes and so has less reason to value closely.
  • Boston’s value basis runs well below the exempt property on its own roll for most institutions: Boston University’s is $2.62 billion against $4.11 billion on the roll. For most institutions it has not changed in five years (above). Every per-$1,000 figure here uses the roll, in both cities, so the comparison measures the same thing on each side.
  • Payments are for fiscal 2025. Boston’s roll is fiscal 2026’s, the first one that values property as of the start of 2025.
  • Harvard’s figures are for its Boston property only, mostly in Allston. What it pays Cambridge is not here.
  • Providence’s rows are the six colleges and hospitals that pay the City. Other exempt owners, and Providence Place, are on Off the Roll.

Not yet on the record

  • How Boston sets each institution’s value basis: which parcels it counts, whether it leaves out student housing, and which year’s values it uses.
  • Three Boston owners are matched by address and history rather than by name: Franciscan Children’s, listed as “Joseph P Kennedy Jr,” its original name; Joslin, listed as “Diabetes Foundation Inc”; and part of MCPHS, listed as “Massachusetts College of.”
