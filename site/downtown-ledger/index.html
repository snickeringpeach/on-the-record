What the city has proposed, and what each needs first
  • RIPTA transit center relocation Proposed 4 years out
    On August 20, 2026 RIPTA's board voted 6 to 0 to take a new transit center into design, engineering and pricing on a 4.4-acre state-owned parcel facing Francis and Gaspee streets, next to Providence Station and across from Providence Place. Contractor Next Wave Partners. Kennedy Plaza would keep reduced service via the Downtown Transit Connector. The governor's schematic shows 16 bus berths curving along the Francis and Gaspee intersection, west of the station and south of the State House. RIPTA will own the site; it is a tenant at Kennedy Plaza, which the City owns. Next Wave's contract is about $16.9 million. The board packet puts the preliminary estimate at $70 million, not final, against $77 million committed ($46 million in the state transportation plan, a $25 million federal BUILD grant, $6 million state match), and says construction would begin within six months of finishing the design contract. No date is given for finishing it; the design and a cost estimate come back to the board first. RIDOT's station page still says 2026 for the station work next door, with no completion announced as of Sept 28.
  • Providence Station renovation Proposed 0 years out
    State of Good Repair and Capacity Project: construction began fall 2024 on a roughly two-year schedule; every official source gives 2026 for completion, and RIDOT said on March 3, 2026 that the work would be complete this year. No completion announcement found as of September 3, 2026. About $30M: $12.5M FRA grant, $9.75M Amtrak, $7.75M RIDOT. Amtrak owns the station.
  • Providence Place tax agreement expiry Proposed 2 years out
    The 1996 tax stabilization agreement runs through 2028 (end of fiscal 2027-28). Under it the mall pays about $500,000 a year in property tax and roughly $1 million a year to the city in all; at the fiscal 2027 commercial rate the bill would be $26.8 million on the current $730 million assessment. But the property sold for $133 million, less than a fifth of that assessment, and would be reassessed before its first full bill, so the cliff is smaller than the headline. The sale closed August 20, 2026 with no tax-deal contingency. Paolino says the owners have no immediate plans to seek a new agreement unless a dramatic development proposal warrants one. Brookfield's 2022 proposed 20-year extension at $4.5 million a year never got a council vote.
  • Costco at Providence Place Proposed years out not yet estimated
    New owner Joe Paolino Jr. is publicly courting Costco; he has also said Costco does not normally do malls and he does not want to give false hope. Rhode Island is one of three states without a Costco; the nearest is Sharon, MA.
  • The green parcel across from the mall (now the RIPTA hub site) Proposed years out not yet estimated
    It is the same lot. The empty, grassed, state-owned parcel west of Providence Station fronting Francis and Gaspee streets, about 4.4 acres, the PVD Fan Zone during the 2026 World Cup, is the parcel RIPTA's board chose on August 20, 2026 for the new transit center. A separate parcel at 150 Francis Street is owned by Amtrak and was studied earlier; Park Row West, on the other side of the station, was Next Wave's higher-ranked site before the Francis Street lot was picked for its footprint.
  • Hospital parking lots in the Jewelry District Proposed years out not yet estimated
    Brown University Health holds 4,280 surface spaces including off-site, more than 40 acres of it in the hospital district. The City Plan Commission approved its Institutional Master Plan in May 2026: the main entrance moves to Plain Street, a 500-space garage is built, Plain and Dudley reconnect. While the garage is built, the hospital will park on the Victory Plating site in the Jewelry District. Marathon's separate seven-story garage at 140 Prairie Ave is paused after neighborhood opposition, and its 251 Blackstone St building was cut from six stories to four. The March 2025 CNU charrette's 2,000-unit proposal on consolidated lots is a proposal, not a plan of record; the approved plan says nothing about housing.
  • Kennedy Plaza after the buses Proposed 4 years out
    Tied to the RIPTA move: Kennedy Plaza would go from the system's hub to a stop with reduced Downtown Transit Connector service. No standalone plan for the plaza found beyond the transit change. Kennedy Plaza is City-owned; RIPTA is a tenant there. Paolino, who owns 100 Westminster overlooking it and now the mall, has said publicly he wants the buses out.
