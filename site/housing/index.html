Providence Permitted 622 New Homes in 2024, or 269, Depending on Which Government Count You Read

The State has set Providence a goal of 3,010 new homes over the next five years. The State’s housing report and the Census Bureau disagree on how many the City permits now, and the State estimates how many get built with a formula instead of a count.

By Liam Freaney · from the Census Bureau, the RI Department of Housing and the City’s own records

The goal

Under Housing 2030, the State’s housing plan, each city and town is expected to permit new homes equal to a share of its housing each year: 0.4 percent for towns without many jobs or much transit, 0.6 percent for towns with more jobs, and 0.8 percent for places with both. Providence is in the third group. Its goal is 3,010 homes from 2026 through 2030, about 602 a year, of which 301 are to be affordable.

RI Department of Housing, Housing 2030 municipal growth categories and production goals (housing.ri.gov/media/3401). Providence’s goal is 0.8 percent of 75,257 homes, times five years.

Two counts of the same permits

Two government surveys count the building permits each city issues for new homes. The Census Bureau’s Building Permits Survey takes what each city reports, month by month. The State’s Department of Housing, renamed the Executive Office of Housing in 2025, surveys each city once a year, and its report says it uses that survey because it is “more accurate than the Census survey.” For Providence in 2024, the Census count is 269 homes and the State count is 622. At the first rate, Providence is permitting 45% of what its goal asks. At the second, it is on pace.

The gap runs statewide. The Census count for all 39 cities and towns in 2024 is 1,757 homes; the State’s is 2,655. In 9 towns the Census figure is partly the Bureau’s own estimate, because the town did not report every month: Coventry, Exeter, Foster, Glocester, Jamestown, Johnston, Lincoln, Newport, Tiverton. The State’s report says its survey covered 38 of the 39 municipalities and used the Census figure for the last one. It does not say which. The Housing 2030 goals are written in permits, and the plan does not say which survey will be used to measure them.

In 12 of the 39 the two counts match exactly. The widest gaps are in Providence, 353 homes, and Pawtucket, 159.

How the State counts what got built

A permit is not a home. To count homes finished, the State’s 2024 report uses a formula. For single-family houses it takes the year before’s permits and multiplies by 0.66, the share of New England single-family permits that become houses within a year in a national Census survey. For apartments it counts certificates of occupancy, but only those reported for developments with affordable units. For Providence that gives 33 houses and 169 apartments, 202 in all. The 169 apartments are four affordable developments: Copley Chambers II and III (124), Joseph Caffey Apartments (39), Portland Homes (5) and one unit at 1192 Westminster St. An apartment building with no affordable units that opened in 2024 is not in the count.

The report says so itself: the method “does not capture the full set of multifamily completions in the state.”

RI Department of Housing, 2024 Integrated Housing Report (Apr. 15, 2025), pp. 5, 16 and 19–20, Figures 2.1 and 2.4.

What Providence told the Census and the State

Both surveys get their numbers from the City. From 2018 through 2024, Providence’s reports to the Census add to 1,038 homes and its answers to the State’s survey add to 3,552. They matched once, in 2022. In 2018 the City reported 1 new home to the Census and 892 to the State; its own published permit records for that year add to 123. In 2023 it reported 5 to the Census, with all 12 months filed, and 1,081 to the State. In 2017, a year it reported 4 homes to the Census, its permit records include a 15-story, 202-apartment building at 169 Canal St.

What got built, parcel by parcel

The Tax Assessor records the year each building went up and how many homes it holds. By that record, 2,559 homes in Providence are in buildings put up from 2010 through 2023, on 819 parcels. That includes 222 the assessor records in student housing; it leaves out 4 hotel parcels. 698 of the homes, 27%, sit on 15 parcels under tax stabilization agreements. In the 14 new buildings with 20 or more homes, not counting student housing, 683 of 1,013 homes, 67%, are on parcels with an agreement.
